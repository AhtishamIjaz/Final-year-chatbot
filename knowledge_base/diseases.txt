MEDICAL CONDITIONS AND DISEASE REFERENCE GUIDE
Source: WHO, CDC, NICE, UpToDate Clinical Guidelines

════════════════════════════════════════
DIABETES MELLITUS
════════════════════════════════════════
Chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose (hyperglycemia) due to defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.

TYPES:
- Type 1 DM: Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta-cells → absolute insulin deficiency. Usually onset in childhood/young adults. Requires lifelong insulin.
- Type 2 DM: Insulin resistance + progressive beta-cell failure. Most common (>90%). Strongly linked to obesity and lifestyle.
- Gestational Diabetes: Occurs during pregnancy. Increases risk of Type 2 in future.
- MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young): Rare genetic form.

SYMPTOMS:
- Classic "3 Polys": Polyuria (frequent urination), Polydipsia (excessive thirst), Polyphagia (excessive hunger)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Blurred vision (osmotic lens changes)
- Fatigue and weakness
- Slow-healing wounds / recurrent infections
- Numbness or tingling in feet (peripheral neuropathy)

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (ADA):
- Fasting blood glucose ≥126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions
- Random blood glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms
- HbA1c ≥6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥200 mg/dL

NORMAL VALUES:
- Fasting: 70–100 mg/dL
- Post-meal (2h): <140 mg/dL
- HbA1c: <5.7% (normal); 5.7–6.4% (prediabetes); ≥6.5% (diabetes)

MANAGEMENT:
- Lifestyle: Medical nutrition therapy (low GI diet), physical activity 150 min/week aerobic
- Medications for Type 2: Metformin (first-line), SGLT-2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, sulfonylureas
- Insulin: Essential for Type 1; used in Type 2 when oral agents insufficient
- Regular monitoring: Blood glucose, HbA1c (every 3 months), kidney function, eye exams, foot exams

COMPLICATIONS:
- Microvascular: Nephropathy (kidney disease), Retinopathy (eye disease), Neuropathy (nerve damage)
- Macrovascular: Coronary artery disease, Stroke, Peripheral arterial disease
- Diabetic foot ulcers and amputation (preventable with foot care)
- Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA): Emergency in Type 1 — fruity breath, vomiting, confusion

════════════════════════════════════════
HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
════════════════════════════════════════
Persistent elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure. Often called "the silent killer" — usually asymptomatic until complications arise.

CLASSIFICATION (ACC/AHA 2017):
- Normal: <120/<80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120–129/<80 mmHg
- Stage 1 HTN: 130–139 / 80–89 mmHg
- Stage 2 HTN: ≥140 / ≥90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: >180/120 mmHg → EMERGENCY

SYMPTOMS (usually none until severe):
- Headaches (usually occipital, morning)
- Visual disturbances
- Nausea
- Hypertensive urgency/emergency: Severe headache, confusion, chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes

PRIMARY HYPERTENSION (Essential): No identifiable cause. ~95% of cases. Linked to genetics, obesity, high salt intake, stress, sedentary lifestyle.

SECONDARY HYPERTENSION: Identifiable cause — renal artery stenosis, primary hyperaldosteronism, obstructive sleep apnea, medications (NSAIDs, oral contraceptives)

MANAGEMENT:
Lifestyle (all stages): DASH diet (low sodium <2.3g/day, rich in fruits/vegetables/low-fat dairy), weight loss (5kg loss ≈ 4 mmHg reduction), exercise 30 min/day, limit alcohol, quit smoking, stress management

Medications:
- First-line: ACE inhibitors (enalapril, lisinopril), ARBs (losartan, valsartan), Calcium Channel Blockers (amlodipine), Thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide)
- Second-line: Beta-blockers, alpha-blockers, spironolactone
- Special populations: Diabetics prefer ACE inhibitor/ARB; Black patients prefer CCB + diuretic

COMPLICATIONS: Stroke, MI, Heart failure, CKD, Hypertensive retinopathy, Aortic dissection

════════════════════════════════════════
INFLUENZA (FLU)
════════════════════════════════════════
Acute viral respiratory illness caused by Influenza A, B, or C viruses. Seasonally epidemic.

SYMPTOMS (sudden onset):
- High fever (38–40°C) with chills
- Severe myalgia (muscle aches) — prominent feature distinguishing from cold
- Headache
- Fatigue (can be profound)
- Dry cough
- Sore throat
- Rhinitis (runny/stuffy nose)
- Some: GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) — more common in children

DIFFERENCES FROM COMMON COLD:
- Flu: Sudden severe onset, high fever, severe aches, extreme fatigue
- Cold: Gradual onset, low/no fever, mild aches, runny nose dominant

TREATMENT:
- Antivirals: Oseltamivir (Tamiflu) 75mg BD x 5 days — best if started within 48h of symptoms. Reduces duration by ~1 day and severity. Especially indicated in high-risk groups.
- Supportive: Rest, fluids, paracetamol for fever, saline nasal rinse

HIGH-RISK GROUPS (complications including pneumonia):
- Age >65 or <5 years
- Pregnant women
- Chronic conditions (diabetes, heart disease, asthma, immunocompromised)

PREVENTION: Annual influenza vaccine (efficacy 40–60%, varies by season); hand hygiene; cough etiquette; mask wearing

SEEK URGENT CARE: Difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, severe or progressive symptoms

════════════════════════════════════════
ASTHMA
════════════════════════════════════════
Chronic inflammatory disease of airways causing recurrent episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and cough.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY: Airway inflammation → bronchospasm + mucus hypersecretion → reversible airflow obstruction

SYMPTOMS:
- Wheezing (high-pitched whistling sound during breathing)
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (especially nocturnal or early morning — diagnostic clue)
- Symptoms often triggered by specific factors; reversible between attacks

TRIGGERS: Allergens (house dust mites, pollen, animal dander, mold), exercise, cold air, respiratory infections, smoke, air pollution, NSAIDs/aspirin, beta-blockers, emotional stress, food additives (sulphites)

SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION:
- Mild intermittent: Symptoms ≤2 days/week, nocturnal ≤2x/month
- Mild persistent: >2 days/week but not daily, nocturnal 3-4x/month
- Moderate persistent: Daily symptoms, nocturnal >1x/week
- Severe persistent: Continuous symptoms, frequent nocturnal, limited activity

TREATMENT (GINA Guidelines):
- Reliever (SABA): Salbutamol (albuterol) inhaler — short-acting bronchodilator for acute relief
- Preventer (ICS): Inhaled corticosteroids (beclomethasone, fluticasone) — reduce inflammation, prevent attacks
- Long-acting: LABA (salmeterol), LAMA (tiotropium), Leukotriene receptor antagonists (montelukast)
- Step-up / step-down therapy based on control

ACUTE ASTHMA ATTACK — MANAGEMENT:
1. Sit upright, stay calm, loosen tight clothing
2. Use salbutamol inhaler via spacer — 4 puffs, wait 1 min, repeat up to 10 puffs
3. If no improvement after 10 minutes → CALL 1122
4. Continue salbutamol (4 puffs every 4 minutes) until ambulance arrives
5. Oxygen if available
6. Oral prednisolone if prescribed

DANGER SIGNS (life-threatening attack): Inability to speak, exhaustion, silent chest (no wheeze heard — air movement too little), blue lips, confusion, SpO2 <92%

════════════════════════════════════════
PNEUMONIA
════════════════════════════════════════
Inflammation of lung tissue (alveoli) caused by infection.

CAUSES: Bacterial (Streptococcus pneumoniae most common), Viral (influenza, RSV, COVID-19), Atypical bacteria (Mycoplasma — "walking pneumonia"), Fungal (Pneumocystis — in immunocompromised)

SYMPTOMS:
- Productive cough with purulent sputum (yellow/green/rust-colored)
- High fever with rigors (shaking chills)
- Pleuritic chest pain (sharp pain worsened by breathing)
- Shortness of breath
- Decreased oxygen saturation
- Confusion (especially elderly — may present atypically)

SEVERITY — CURB-65 Score (1 point each): Confusion, Urea >7mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30, Blood pressure <90 systolic, Age ≥65
- 0–1: Outpatient treatment
- 2: Inpatient consideration
- ≥3: ICU consideration

TREATMENT:
- Community-acquired pneumonia: Amoxicillin ± macrolide (azithromycin) outpatient; IV antibiotics inpatient
- Rest, fluids, oxygen if hypoxic
- Hospitalization for severe cases

PREVENTION: Pneumococcal vaccine (PCV13, PPSV23); Influenza vaccine; Smoking cessation

════════════════════════════════════════
COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)
════════════════════════════════════════
SYMPTOMS: Fever, cough, shortness of breath, fatigue, body aches, headache, loss of taste/smell, sore throat, runny nose, nausea/diarrhea

EMERGENCY SYMPTOMS → Call 1122:
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Confusion or difficulty staying awake
- Trouble breathing (SpO2 <94%)
- Bluish lips or face

MANAGEMENT: Isolation, rest, fluids, paracetamol for fever. Antivirals (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, remdesivir) for high-risk patients. Dexamethasone for severe disease requiring oxygen.

PREVENTION: Vaccination series + boosters; Masks (high-risk settings); Hand hygiene; Ventilation.

════════════════════════════════════════
DENGUE FEVER
════════════════════════════════════════
Viral disease transmitted by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. Endemic in Pakistan.

PHASES:
1. Febrile phase (days 1-3): High fever (39-40°C), severe headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia ("breakbone fever"), flushed face
2. Critical phase (days 4-6): Fever may drop — WARNING PHASE — plasma leakage occurs. Dengue warning signs appear.
3. Recovery phase (days 7-10): Reabsorption, fluid overload risk

DENGUE WARNING SIGNS (Hospitalize immediately):
- Severe abdominal pain or tenderness
- Persistent vomiting
- Clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion)
- Mucosal bleeding
- Lethargy or restlessness
- Liver enlargement >2cm
- Rapid decline in platelet count

SEVERE DENGUE (Emergency): Severe plasma leakage → shock; Severe bleeding; Severe organ impairment (liver, CNS, kidneys)

MANAGEMENT:
- No specific antiviral
- PARACETAMOL ONLY for fever — NOT ibuprofen or aspirin (increases bleeding risk)
- Aggressive oral/IV hydration
- Monitor platelet count and haematocrit daily during critical phase
- Platelet transfusion only if count <10,000 or significant bleeding

PREVENTION: Eliminate Aedes mosquito breeding sites (stagnant water); Window screens; DEET repellent; Long-sleeved clothing; Indoor spraying

════════════════════════════════════════
MALARIA
════════════════════════════════════════
Parasitic disease caused by Plasmodium species; transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes.

SPECIES IN PAKISTAN: P. vivax (most common), P. falciparum (most dangerous)

SYMPTOMS (appear 10-15 days post-bite):
- Classic triad: Fever, Chills (rigors), Sweating — may be cyclical (every 48 or 72 hours)
- Headache, myalgia, nausea/vomiting
- Splenomegaly with chronic infection

SEVERE MALARIA (P. falciparum — Emergency):
- Cerebral malaria: Altered consciousness, seizures, coma
- Severe anemia (Hb <7 g/dL)
- Acute respiratory distress
- Hypoglycemia, metabolic acidosis
- Acute kidney injury (blackwater fever — haemoglobinuria)

DIAGNOSIS: Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) + Blood film (thick and thin smear). Parasite density determines severity.

TREATMENT:
- Uncomplicated P. vivax: Chloroquine + Primaquine (must check G6PD status first)
- Uncomplicated P. falciparum: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) — artemether + lumefantrine
- Severe malaria: IV artesunate (preferred over quinine)

PREVENTION: Insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs); Indoor residual spraying; Chemoprophylaxis for travelers; Elimination of breeding sites; Prompt diagnosis and treatment

════════════════════════════════════════
TUBERCULOSIS (TB)
════════════════════════════════════════
Caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Pakistan is a high-burden country (5th globally).

TYPES:
- Latent TB infection (LTBI): Bacteria dormant, no symptoms, not contagious but can reactivate
- Active TB disease: Bacteria multiplying, symptomatic, contagious

PULMONARY TB SYMPTOMS (most common):
- Persistent cough ≥2 weeks (productive, blood-stained — haemoptysis)
- Low-grade fever (afternoon rise)
- Drenching night sweats
- Unexplained weight loss
- Chest pain
- Fatigue

EXTRA-PULMONARY TB: Can affect lymph nodes (scrofula), spine (Pott's disease), meninges (TB meningitis), genitourinary system, abdomen

DIAGNOSIS: Sputum smear microscopy, GeneXpert MTB/RIF, culture, CXR (upper lobe infiltrates/cavities), Mantoux test/IGRA (latent TB)

TREATMENT — DOTS Therapy:
- Intensive phase (2 months): RHEZ — Rifampicin + Isoniazid + Ethambutol + Pyrazinamide
- Continuation phase (4 months): RH — Rifampicin + Isoniazid
- Drug-resistant TB (MDR-TB, XDR-TB) requires specialist management with second-line drugs (18-24 months)
- Never miss doses — incomplete treatment causes drug resistance

PREVENTION: BCG vaccination at birth; Early case detection and treatment; Proper ventilation; Contact tracing

════════════════════════════════════════
CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASES
════════════════════════════════════════
Leading cause of death worldwide.

CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (CAD):
- Atherosclerotic plaque narrows coronary arteries → reduced blood flow to heart muscle
- Stable Angina: Chest pain on exertion, relieved by rest/nitrates
- Unstable Angina/NSTEMI/STEMI: Acute coronary syndromes — EMERGENCY

HEART ATTACK RECOGNITION — EMERGENCY:
Symptoms: Central crushing chest pain; Radiation to left arm, jaw, neck, or back; Sweating (diaphoresis); Nausea/vomiting; Shortness of breath; Sense of impending doom
CALL 1122 IMMEDIATELY — Time is muscle (door-to-balloon <90 min target)
- Give aspirin 300mg if available and no allergy
- Position patient comfortably; reassure

HEART FAILURE:
- Heart cannot pump sufficient blood. Symptoms: Breathlessness (exertional, orthopnoea, PND), peripheral oedema (ankle swelling), fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance
- Management: Diuretics, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, spironolactone, SGLT-2 inhibitors, cardiac resynchronization therapy

STROKE — BRAIN ATTACK — EMERGENCY:
FAST acronym: Face drooping + Arm weakness + Speech difficulty → Time to call 1122
Every minute counts — IV thrombolysis within 4.5 hours; Mechanical thrombectomy within 24 hours for large vessel occlusion

════════════════════════════════════════
MENTAL HEALTH CONDITIONS
════════════════════════════════════════

DEPRESSION (Major Depressive Disorder):
- Symptoms: Persistent low mood ≥2 weeks, anhedonia (loss of pleasure), fatigue, sleep disturbance (insomnia or hypersomnia), appetite change, poor concentration, feelings of worthlessness/guilt, psychomotor changes, recurrent thoughts of death
- PHQ-9 score used for screening and severity
- Treatment: CBT (first-line for mild-moderate); SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram) for moderate-severe; combined therapy for severe
- Crisis: If suicidal ideation — immediate mental health crisis line or emergency department

ANXIETY DISORDERS:
- GAD: Excessive, uncontrollable worry about multiple domains; must be ≥6 months
- Panic Disorder: Recurrent unexpected panic attacks — sudden intense fear with palpitations, trembling, shortness of breath, feeling of unreality, fear of dying
- Social Anxiety Disorder: Fear of social situations; significant impairment
- Treatment: CBT (gold standard); SSRIs/SNRIs; benzodiazepines (short-term); buspirone for GAD; exposure therapy

════════════════════════════════════════
ANEMIA
════════════════════════════════════════
Reduced RBC count or hemoglobin concentration, impairing oxygen delivery.

Normal Hb: Males ≥13 g/dL; Females ≥12 g/dL; Pregnant ≥11 g/dL

TYPES:
- Iron-deficiency anemia (most common globally): Microcytic, hypochromic. Causes: Poor dietary intake, blood loss (menstruation, GI bleed, hookworm), malabsorption. Treatment: Ferrous sulphate 200mg TDS + vitamin C.
- Vitamin B12 / Folate deficiency (Megaloblastic anemia): Macrocytic. Causes: Poor diet (vegans), malabsorption, metformin use. Treatment: IM B12 injections or high-dose oral; Folic acid 5mg.
- Hemolytic anemia: Premature RBC destruction — sickle cell disease, thalassemia, G6PD deficiency, autoimmune
- Aplastic anemia: Bone marrow failure — specialist management

SYMPTOMS: Fatigue, weakness, pallor (pale skin/conjunctiva/nailbeds), breathlessness on exertion, palpitations, dizziness, cold hands/feet, koilonychia (iron deficiency), glossitis, angular stomatitis
