FIRST AID EMERGENCY GUIDE
Source: British Red Cross, St. John Ambulance, WHO First Aid Guidelines

⚠️ IMPORTANT: First aid is the immediate help given until professional medical care arrives. Always call emergency services (1122) for life-threatening emergencies.

════════════════════════════════════════
BASIC LIFE SUPPORT (BLS) / CPR
════════════════════════════════════════
When to start CPR: Person is unresponsive AND not breathing normally

ADULT CPR SEQUENCE (AED + CPR):
1. SAFE — Check scene is safe for you and casualty
2. STIMULATE — Shake shoulders and shout "Are you alright?"
3. SHOUT for help — Ask bystander to call 1122 and get AED
4. AIRWAY — Tilt head back, lift chin to open airway
5. BREATHING — Look, listen, feel for breathing for 10 seconds
6. If not breathing:
   - Chest compressions: Place heel of hand on center of chest; 30 compressions at rate of 100–120/min; depth 5–6 cm; allow full recoil
   - Rescue breaths: 2 breaths (1 second each, watch chest rise)
   - Continue 30:2 ratio until AED arrives, help arrives, or you are exhausted

AED USE: Turn on → Follow voice prompts → Deliver shock if advised → Immediately resume CPR

CHILD CPR (1 year to puberty):
- 5 rescue breaths first (drowning/airway primary)
- Then 30 compressions : 2 breaths
- Use 1–2 fingers or one hand for compressions

INFANT CPR (under 1 year):
- 5 rescue breaths (mouth over nose and mouth)
- 30 compressions using 2 fingers on center of chest; depth 4cm
- 30:2 ratio

════════════════════════════════════════
CHOKING (AIRWAY OBSTRUCTION)
════════════════════════════════════════
MILD CHOKING (can speak/cough):
- Encourage to keep coughing
- Do NOT slap back if they are coughing effectively
- Stay with them and monitor

SEVERE CHOKING (cannot speak, breathe, or cough — may be silent):
ADULT / CHILD (over 1 year):
1. Give up to 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand
2. Check mouth after each — remove visible obstruction (blind finger sweeps not recommended)
3. If unsuccessful: Give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)
   - Stand behind person, arms around waist
   - Make fist between navel and lower chest
   - Thrust sharply inward and upward
4. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts
5. If loses consciousness: Start CPR — chest compressions may dislodge object

PREGNANT WOMEN / OBESE: Use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts

INFANT (under 1 year):
1. Face-down across forearm, support head lower than chest
2. 5 back blows between shoulder blades (heel of hand)
3. Turn face-up, 5 chest thrusts with 2 fingers on sternum
4. Check mouth; repeat until clear or unconscious

════════════════════════════════════════
BURNS AND SCALDS
════════════════════════════════════════
DEGREES:
- First degree (superficial): Red, painful, dry — like sunburn
- Second degree (partial thickness): Blistering, very painful
- Third degree (full thickness): White/charred/leathery, may not be painful (nerve destruction)

IMMEDIATE FIRST AID (all burns):
1. COOL the burn with cool (NOT cold/ice) running water for 20 MINUTES — WITHIN 3 HOURS of injury
2. REMOVE clothing/jewellery around burn (NOT if stuck to skin)
3. COVER with cling film (plastic wrap) or clean non-fluffy material — not cotton wool
4. SEEK MEDICAL HELP

DO NOT:
- Apply butter, toothpaste, oils, ice — these worsen injury
- Burst blisters (infection risk)
- Use fluffy bandages

WHEN TO CALL 1122:
- Burns >3cm diameter
- Burns on face, hands, feet, genitals, joints
- Chemical or electrical burns
- Inhalation injury (singed nasal hair, hoarse voice, soot)
- Signs of shock
- Third-degree burns (any size)
- Burns in elderly, children, immunocompromised

CHEMICAL BURNS: Brush off dry chemical first; irrigate with water for 30–60 minutes; remove contaminated clothing; do NOT neutralize acids with alkalis or vice versa

════════════════════════════════════════
BLEEDING / HAEMORRHAGE CONTROL
════════════════════════════════════════
EXTERNAL BLEEDING:
1. Apply DIRECT PRESSURE — use a clean cloth, bandage, or your hand; press firmly
2. Do NOT remove dressing if soaked through — add more on top
3. ELEVATE the injured limb above heart level (if no fracture)
4. Maintain pressure for at least 10 minutes without lifting to check
5. Secure with bandage when bleeding controlled

SEVERE BLEEDING (life-threatening):
- Apply firm direct pressure
- If on limb and pressure not controlling: Tourniquet — apply 5–7cm above wound, tight enough to stop bleeding; note time applied
- Pack wounds: For deep wounds (knife, gunshot), pack tightly with gauze/cloth
- Call 1122 immediately

DO NOT remove embedded objects — stabilize around them

INTERNAL BLEEDING — suspect if:
- Mechanism of significant trauma
- Abdominal rigidity/distension
- Signs of shock (pale, cold, sweaty, rapid pulse, low BP, confusion)
- Bruising without clear cause
- Blood from natural orifices
Action: Call 1122; lay flat with legs elevated (shock position unless spinal injury suspected); keep warm; reassure; nothing by mouth

NOSEBLEED (Epistaxis):
1. Sit forward (prevent swallowing blood)
2. Pinch soft part of nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing
3. Breathe through mouth
4. Ice pack on back of neck may help
5. If bleeding continues >20 minutes or heavy → emergency department

════════════════════════════════════════
HEAD INJURY
════════════════════════════════════════
CONCERN SIGNS — Seek immediate medical care:
- Loss of consciousness (even brief)
- Confusion or disorientation
- Repeated vomiting after head injury
- Worsening headache
- Seizure after head injury
- Clear fluid from nose or ears (CSF leak)
- Unequal pupils
- Weakness or numbness in limbs
- Slurred speech
- Any head injury in child under 2 years

For SUSPECTED SPINAL INJURY:
- Do NOT move patient unless in immediate danger
- Keep head and neck immobilized (manual in-line stabilization)
- Call 1122

For CONCUSSION (mild TBI):
- Rest from physical and cognitive activity (screen time, work, school)
- Gradual return to activity guided by symptoms
- No alcohol or sedating medications
- Not safe to drive — seek medical clearance

════════════════════════════════════════
FRACTURES AND SPRAINS
════════════════════════════════════════
FRACTURE (Broken Bone) signs: Pain, swelling, deformity, bruising, inability to use limb, abnormal movement or sound

FIRST AID:
1. Do NOT try to straighten or realign
2. IMMOBILIZE — splint in position found; improvised splint using padded rigid material; bandage to adjacent limb/body
3. ICE — apply wrapped ice pack to reduce swelling (do not apply directly to skin)
4. ELEVATE if possible
5. Seek medical care for X-ray

OPEN FRACTURE (bone through skin):
- Cover wound with clean dressing — do NOT push bone back
- Control bleeding around bone (do not press on bone)
- Emergency department immediately

SPRAIN (ligament injury) vs STRAIN (muscle/tendon injury):
RICE protocol:
- Rest — avoid activity that causes pain
- Ice — 20 minutes every 2 hours for first 48 hours
- Compression — elastic bandage to reduce swelling
- Elevation — above heart level

════════════════════════════════════════
ANAPHYLAXIS (SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION)
════════════════════════════════════════
EMERGENCY — Can be fatal within minutes. Call 1122 immediately.

SIGNS: Sudden onset following trigger (food, medication, bee sting); urticaria/angioedema; throat tightening/stridor; difficulty breathing/wheeze; hypotension/shock; rapid pulse; nausea/vomiting; pale/clammy; confusion

TREATMENT:
1. ADRENALINE (Epinephrine) — FIRST AND MOST IMPORTANT: 0.5mg (0.5mL of 1:1000) IM into outer mid-thigh; EpiPen/Jext autoinjector
2. Call 1122
3. Lay flat with legs elevated (or upright if breathing difficulty)
4. Second dose adrenaline after 5 minutes if no improvement
5. Supplemental oxygen if available
6. IV fluids if trained and available
7. Hospital observation minimum 6–12 hours (biphasic reaction risk)

Common triggers: Peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, shellfish, medications (penicillin, NSAIDs), bee/wasp stings, latex

════════════════════════════════════════
DIABETIC EMERGENCIES
════════════════════════════════════════
HYPOGLYCAEMIA (Low Blood Sugar <3.5 mmol/L / <63 mg/dL):
Symptoms: Trembling, sweating, hunger, pallor, palpitations, confusion, dizziness, aggression, seizure, unconsciousness

CONSCIOUS PATIENT:
1. Give fast-acting sugar: 15–20g glucose → 150mL orange juice / 3 glucose tablets / 5 jelly babies / 4 teaspoons sugar
2. Wait 15 minutes — re-check blood glucose
3. If still low — repeat
4. Once normalized: Give starchy snack (bread, biscuits) to prevent rebound

UNCONSCIOUS/UNABLE TO SWALLOW:
1. DO NOT give anything by mouth (aspiration risk)
2. Recovery position
3. Glucagon injection 1mg IM/SC if available and trained
4. Call 1122 for IV dextrose administration

HYPERGLYCAEMIA / DKA (High blood sugar — usually Type 1 diabetics):
Signs: Excessive thirst, polyuria, abdominal pain, vomiting, fruity breath (ketones), drowsiness, Kussmaul breathing (deep rapid breathing)
Action: Call 1122; insulin and IV fluids in hospital emergency; potentially fatal if untreated

════════════════════════════════════════
HEAT STROKE AND HEAT EXHAUSTION
════════════════════════════════════════
HEAT EXHAUSTION (core temp 37–40°C):
Symptoms: Heavy sweating, cool/pale/clammy skin, fast/weak pulse, nausea, muscle cramps, dizziness, fatigue

First aid:
1. Move to cool environment
2. Remove excess clothing
3. Lay flat, elevate legs
4. Cool with water spray and fan; cool wet towels on skin
5. Oral rehydration (water, ORS) if conscious
6. Seek medical attention

HEAT STROKE (core temp >40°C — EMERGENCY):
Symptoms: HOT/RED/DRY or wet skin, rapid strong pulse, confusion, dizziness, slurred speech, seizures, unconsciousness

1. CALL 1122 immediately
2. Cool immediately by any means: ice/cold packs to neck/groin/axillae; cool water immersion if possible; wet sheet + fan
3. Do not give oral fluids if confusion present
4. Recovery position if unconscious and breathing normally
5. Target temperature <38.5°C before transport

════════════════════════════════════════
STROKE / SUSPECTED STROKE
════════════════════════════════════════
FAST RECOGNITION:
- F — Face: Ask them to smile — does one side droop?
- A — Arms: Ask to raise both arms — does one drift downward?
- S — Speech: Ask to repeat "The sun is shining" — is speech slurred/strange?
- T — Time: If YES to any → Call 1122 immediately — NOTE time of symptom onset

FIRST AID:
1. Call 1122 IMMEDIATELY
2. Note exact time symptoms started (critical for thrombolysis eligibility)
3. Lay patient down with head and shoulders slightly raised
4. Do NOT give food or water (swallowing may be impaired)
5. If unconscious and breathing: Recovery position
6. Loosen tight clothing
7. Reassure and stay with them until help arrives

════════════════════════════════════════
POISONING / OVERDOSE
════════════════════════════════════════
1. Ensure scene safety (especially for gas/fume poisoning)
2. Call 1122 and poison control
3. If conscious: Keep awake and talking; do NOT induce vomiting unless specifically advised
4. If unconscious and breathing: Recovery position
5. If not breathing: CPR

INFORMATION TO GIVE TO EMERGENCY SERVICES:
- Substance taken (bring container if possible)
- Amount taken
- Time of ingestion
- Age and weight of patient
- Current symptoms

Specific: PARACETAMOL OVERDOSE: Even if patient feels fine, requires urgent hospital treatment; antidote (N-acetylcysteine) must be given promptly. Do not wait for symptoms.

CARBON MONOXIDE POISONING: Remove from exposure immediately to fresh air; call 1122; headache + nausea + confusion in multiple people in same building = CO until proven otherwise; do not re-enter building
