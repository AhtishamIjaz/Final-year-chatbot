SYMPTOMS AND PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT GUIDE
Source: WHO Clinical Guidelines, CDC Medical Reference, NICE Guidelines

════════════════════════════════════════
HEADACHE
════════════════════════════════════════
A headache is pain or discomfort in the head, scalp, or neck.

TENSION HEADACHE (Most Common):
- Symptoms: Dull, aching head pain; feeling of tightness/pressure across forehead, sides, or back of head; tenderness in scalp, neck, shoulders
- Causes: Stress, anxiety, poor posture, eye strain, dehydration, lack of sleep
- Treatment: Rest, hydration, OTC pain relievers (paracetamol 500-1000mg, ibuprofen 400mg), relaxation techniques
- Duration: 30 minutes to several hours

MIGRAINE:
- Symptoms: Severe throbbing/pulsing pain (usually one side), nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light and sound, visual aura (flashes, zigzag lines)
- Triggers: Hormonal changes, certain foods (chocolate, caffeine, aged cheese, alcohol), stress, bright lights, strong smells, sleep changes
- Duration: 4–72 hours
- Treatment: Prescribed triptans, rest in dark quiet room, cold/warm compress, anti-nausea medication
- Seek emergency care if: Worst headache of your life (thunderclap), sudden severe onset, headache with fever + stiff neck (meningitis sign), after head injury, with confusion or vision changes

CLUSTER HEADACHE:
- Severe pain around one eye, occurring in clusters (several times per day)
- Accompanied by red eye, nasal congestion on affected side
- Requires specialist treatment

FEVER (PYREXIA)
════════════════════════════════════════
Normal body temperature: 36.1–37.2°C (97–99°F)
Fever defined as: ≥38°C (100.4°F)

CAUSES:
- Viral infections (influenza, COVID-19, dengue)
- Bacterial infections (pneumonia, UTI, sepsis)
- Inflammatory conditions (rheumatoid arthritis)
- Heat exhaustion or heat stroke
- Certain medications

GRADING:
- Low-grade fever: 37.3–38°C
- Moderate fever: 38–39°C  
- High fever: 39–40°C
- Hyperpyrexia (Emergency): >40°C (104°F)

HOME MANAGEMENT:
- Rest and plenty of fluids (water, ORS, clear soups)
- Paracetamol (acetaminophen) — 500-1000mg every 4-6 hours (adults)
- Ibuprofen — 400-600mg every 6-8 hours with food (not for children under 12 months)
- Light clothing and bedding; cool environment
- Tepid sponging if very high fever
- Do NOT give aspirin to children (risk of Reye's syndrome)

EMERGENCY — SEEK IMMEDIATE CARE:
- Temperature above 40°C (104°F) in adults
- Any fever in infant under 3 months
- Fever with severe headache + stiff neck + sensitivity to light (meningitis)
- Fever with difficulty breathing
- Fever with purple/red rash that doesn't fade under pressure (meningococcal)
- Fever with confusion or altered consciousness
- Fever lasting more than 5 days without improvement

COUGH
════════════════════════════════════════

TYPES:
- Dry (non-productive) cough: No mucus. Common in viral infections, asthma, allergies, COVID-19, GERD
- Productive (wet) cough: Produces mucus/phlegm. Common in chest infections, bronchitis, pneumonia
- Chronic cough: Lasting >8 weeks. Needs medical evaluation (GERD, asthma, post-nasal drip, medications like ACE inhibitors)

COMMON CAUSES:
1. Common cold / upper respiratory tract infection
2. Influenza
3. COVID-19
4. Asthma (especially night cough)
5. Allergies / hay fever
6. Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
7. Postnasal drip from sinusitis
8. Smoking
9. Tuberculosis (chronic cough with blood)

HOME MANAGEMENT:
- Stay well hydrated
- Honey and warm water or lemon tea (soothe throat)
- Cough drops or lozenges
- Steam inhalation for congestion
- Saline nasal rinse for postnasal drip
- Humidifier in bedroom
- Avoid irritants: smoke, dust, cold air

SEEK MEDICAL ATTENTION IF:
- Cough lasting more than 3 weeks
- Coughing up blood (haemoptysis) — URGENT
- Cough with shortness of breath or wheezing
- Cough with high fever (>39°C)
- Cough with significant weight loss (TB risk)
- Cough in smoker over 40 (lung cancer screening)

CHEST PAIN
════════════════════════════════════════
⚠️ MEDICAL EMERGENCY — Chest pain can indicate a life-threatening condition.
Call emergency services (1122) immediately if you have chest pain.

CARDIAC CAUSES (Emergency):
- Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction): Crushing/squeezing chest pain, radiating to left arm, jaw, neck or back; sweating; nausea; shortness of breath; sense of doom
- Angina: Chest tightness triggered by exertion, relieved by rest. Requires immediate medical attention
- Aortic Dissection: Sudden severe tearing pain radiating to back

NON-CARDIAC CAUSES (Still require evaluation):
- Costochondritis: Sharp pain worsened by pressing on chest wall
- Pleurisy: Sharp pain worsened by deep breathing or coughing (lung lining inflammation)
- Pneumonia / Pulmonary Embolism: Chest pain with fever/shortness of breath
- GERD / Acid Reflux: Burning pain, often after meals, worsened lying down
- Anxiety / Panic Attack: Tight chest with rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, dizziness
- Muscle strain: Pain worsened by movement, tenderness on palpation

NEVER IGNORE CHEST PAIN. Always seek immediate medical evaluation.

════════════════════════════════════════
SHORTNESS OF BREATH (DYSPNOEA)
════════════════════════════════════════
EMERGENCY — Call 1122 immediately if:
- Sudden severe difficulty breathing
- Shortness of breath with chest pain (heart attack)
- Blue-tinged lips or fingertips (cyanosis) — critical oxygen deprivation
- Cannot speak full sentences
- Breathing rate >30/minute in adults

CAUSES:
- Asthma attack
- Anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction)
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary embolism (blood clot in lungs)
- Heart failure
- COVID-19
- Panic attack / anxiety
- Severe anemia
- COPD exacerbation

════════════════════════════════════════
ABDOMINAL (STOMACH) PAIN
════════════════════════════════════════
Location clues:
- Right upper quadrant: Liver, gallbladder (gallstones, hepatitis)
- Left upper quadrant: Stomach, spleen (gastritis, pancreatitis)
- Right lower quadrant: Appendix ⚠️ (appendicitis), ovary (women)
- Left lower quadrant: Colon (diverticulitis), ovary (women)
- Central/umbilical: Early appendicitis, intestinal obstruction
- Diffuse: Gastroenteritis, IBS, peritonitis

EMERGENCY SIGNS:
- Sudden severe "worst pain ever" — possible ruptured organ or aneurysm
- Rigid/board-like abdomen — peritonitis
- Pain migrating from navel to lower right + fever + nausea (appendicitis)
- Vomiting blood or passing black/tarry stool
- Signs of shock (pale, sweating, low blood pressure, rapid pulse)

COMMON NON-EMERGENCY CAUSES:
- Indigestion/gas: Bloating, belching, mild discomfort after eating
- Gastroenteritis: Cramping with diarrhea and/or vomiting
- Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS): Chronic intermittent pain with altered bowel habits
- Menstrual cramps: Lower abdominal cramping during menstruation
- Kidney stones: Severe flank pain radiating to groin, blood in urine

════════════════════════════════════════
NAUSEA AND VOMITING
════════════════════════════════════════
CAUSES: Food poisoning, gastroenteritis, pregnancy (morning sickness), motion sickness, migraine, medications, anxiety, inner ear problems

HOME MANAGEMENT:
- Small, frequent sips of clear fluids (water, ORS, ginger ale)
- BRAT diet when tolerated: Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, Toast
- Ginger (tea or capsules) shown effective for nausea
- Avoid solid food until nausea settles
- Anti-nausea medications: metoclopramide, ondansetron (prescribed)

SEEK MEDICAL CARE IF:
- Vomiting persistently for more than 24 hours
- Signs of dehydration: dry mouth, decreased urine output, dizziness
- Blood in vomit (haematemesis) — URGENT
- Severe abdominal pain with vomiting
- Vomiting after head injury (concussion)

════════════════════════════════════════
DIARRHEA
════════════════════════════════════════
Defined as 3+ loose/watery stools per day.

CAUSES: Viral gastroenteritis (most common), bacterial food poisoning, parasites, antibiotics (C. diff), IBS, IBD (Crohn's, Ulcerative Colitis), food intolerance (lactose, gluten)

MANAGEMENT:
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) — critical to prevent dehydration
- BRAT diet
- Probiotics (Lactobacillus) can reduce duration
- Avoid dairy, fatty foods, high-fiber foods, caffeine during acute episode
- Antibiotics only if bacterial cause confirmed (e.g., cholera, Salmonella)

SEEK MEDICAL CARE IF:
- Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days in adults
- Blood or mucus in stool
- Severe abdominal pain
- High fever (>38.5°C)
- Signs of severe dehydration (no urination >8 hours, confusion)
- Recent travel to developing country (traveler's diarrhea — possible parasite)

════════════════════════════════════════
SORE THROAT (PHARYNGITIS)
════════════════════════════════════════
VIRAL (Most common — ~70%): Cold, flu, mononucleosis. No antibiotics needed. Self-limiting.

BACTERIAL — STREP THROAT (Group A Streptococcus):
Signs suggesting strep (see a doctor for throat swab):
- Severe throat pain + difficulty swallowing
- White patches/pus on tonsils
- Fever >38°C
- Swollen tender lymph nodes in neck
- ABSENCE of cough (cough suggests viral cause)
Treatment: Amoxicillin or penicillin 10-day course (must complete to prevent rheumatic fever)

HOME REMEDIES:
- Warm salt water gargle (1/4 tsp salt in 8oz warm water)
- Honey and warm lemon water
- Throat lozenges containing benzocaine or menthol
- Cold fluids or ice chips (soothing)
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain and fever

════════════════════════════════════════
FATIGUE AND WEAKNESS
════════════════════════════════════════
CAUSES:
Medical: Anemia, hypothyroidism, diabetes, heart failure, kidney/liver disease, cancer, infections (TB, hepatitis, HIV), sleep apnea, vitamin D/B12 deficiency
Mental health: Depression, anxiety, burnout
Lifestyle: Poor sleep, physical inactivity, poor nutrition, dehydration, excessive alcohol

WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR:
- Fatigue lasting more than 2 weeks without obvious cause
- Fatigue with significant unexplained weight loss
- Extreme fatigue with shortness of breath or chest pain
- Fatigue with fever, night sweats (TB, malignancy)
- Fatigue with depression or suicidal thoughts

ASSESSMENT: Doctor may check CBC, thyroid function (TSH), blood glucose, vitamin levels, kidney/liver function

════════════════════════════════════════
DIZZINESS AND VERTIGO
════════════════════════════════════════
TYPES:
- Vertigo: Sensation of spinning (inner ear — BPPV, labyrinthitis, Meniere's disease)
- Presyncope: Feeling faint, about to pass out (low blood pressure, dehydration, anemia, cardiac)
- Disequilibrium: Unsteadiness when walking (neurological)

EMERGENCY — Possible stroke — FAST test:
- F: Face drooping
- A: Arm weakness
- S: Speech difficulty
- T: Time to call 1122

Emergency also if dizziness is accompanied by:
- Sudden severe headache
- Double vision or loss of vision
- Difficulty swallowing or speaking
- Chest pain or palpitations

BENIGN POSITIONAL VERTIGO (BPPV):
- Spinning sensation triggered by head position changes
- Epley manoeuvre (repositioning technique) is highly effective
- Usually resolves within weeks

════════════════════════════════════════
SKIN RASH
════════════════════════════════════════
TYPES:
- Urticaria (Hives): Itchy raised welts, allergic reaction — antihistamines
- Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis): Chronic itchy inflamed skin — moisturizers, topical steroids
- Contact Dermatitis: Rash from skin contact with allergen/irritant
- Psoriasis: Scaly silvery plaques — specialist treatment required
- Chickenpox: Blistering spots with fever — Varicella virus
- Shingles: Painful blistering rash along nerve distribution — antiviral treatment
- Ringworm (Tinea): Circular scaling patch — antifungal cream
- Measles: Red spots + fever + cough + runny nose + conjunctivitis

EMERGENCY:
- Rash with difficulty breathing / throat swelling — anaphylaxis (1122 immediately, use adrenaline if available)
- Purple/red non-blanching spots (meningococcal disease — medical emergency)

════════════════════════════════════════
JOINT AND BACK PAIN
════════════════════════════════════════
BACK PAIN — Common causes:
- Muscle or ligament strain (most common — rest + physiotherapy)
- Herniated/slipped disc — pain radiating down leg (sciatica)
- Arthritis
- Spinal stenosis
- Kidney problems (flank pain)

Back pain RED FLAGS (seek urgent care):
- Pain after significant trauma
- Loss of bladder or bowel control (cauda equina — EMERGENCY)
- Numbness or tingling in both legs
- Unexplained weight loss + back pain (possible malignancy)
- Fever with back pain (spinal infection)
- Pain not improving after 6 weeks of conservative treatment

JOINT PAIN — Common causes:
- Osteoarthritis: Wear-and-tear, stiffness worse in morning and after rest
- Rheumatoid arthritis: Symmetrical joint inflammation, morning stiffness >1 hour
- Gout: Sudden severe pain, redness, warmth — usually big toe
- Septic arthritis: Infected joint — EMERGENCY (fever + hot swollen joint)

URINARY SYMPTOMS:
Urinary Tract Infection (UTI): Burning urination, frequency, urgency, cloudy/smelly urine, pelvic pain. More common in women. Treatment: antibiotics + increased fluid intake.
Kidney Stones: Severe colicky flank pain radiating to groin, blood in urine, nausea. Pain is one of the most severe experienced.
