CLINICAL GUIDELINES AND PREVENTIVE HEALTH RECOMMENDATIONS
Source: WHO Global Health Guidelines, CDC Clinical Recommendations, NICE Guidelines, Pakistan Ministry of Health

════════════════════════════════════════
PREVENTIVE HEALTH AND SCREENING
════════════════════════════════════════

ROUTINE HEALTH SCREENINGS BY AGE:

ALL ADULTS:
- Blood pressure: Measured at every medical visit (≥18 years); screen for hypertension
- Blood glucose (diabetes screening): Every 3 years for adults 45+ or earlier if overweight with risk factors
- Cholesterol (lipid profile): Starting at age 20; every 4–6 years if normal
- BMI/Weight assessment: At every medical visit
- Dental checkup: Every 6–12 months
- Eye examination: Every 1–2 years (more frequent with diabetes or family history of glaucoma)
- Hearing assessment: Every 10 years from age 18, every 3 years from age 50

WOMEN-SPECIFIC SCREENINGS:
- Cervical cancer (Pap smear): Every 3 years from age 21–65; or every 5 years with HPV co-test (21–65)
- Breast cancer (mammogram): Every 1–2 years from age 40–74 (discuss with doctor)
- Bone density (DEXA scan): Women 65+ or postmenopausal women with risk factors
- Anemia screening: During pregnancy (all trimesters)
- Gestational diabetes: All pregnant women at 24–28 weeks (OGTT)

MEN-SPECIFIC SCREENINGS:
- Prostate cancer (PSA): Discuss with doctor at age 50 (earlier if high risk or African-American)
- Testicular self-examination: Monthly from late teens

CHILDREN AND ADOLESCENTS:
- Growth monitoring and developmental milestones
- Vision and hearing screening at school entry
- Anemia screening: At 9 and 18 months
- Blood pressure: From age 3 at every well-child visit
- Dental: First visit by age 1

NUTRITION AND DIETARY GUIDELINES
════════════════════════════════════════
WHO recommends a HEALTHY DIET for all adults:

MACRONUTRIENTS:
- Fruits and vegetables: At least 400g (5 portions) per day; reduces risk of NCDs
- Whole grains: At least half of grain intake should be whole grain (brown rice, whole wheat, oats)
- Protein: Varied sources — legumes (lentils, chickpeas), lean meats, fish, eggs, nuts
- Healthy fats: Unsaturated fats (olive oil, canola, avocado, nuts, fish) preferred over saturated fats

REDUCE:
- Free sugars: <10% of total energy intake (<50g/day for adults); ideally <5% for health benefits
- Sodium (salt): <5g per day (<2g sodium) to reduce hypertension risk; avoid processed foods, use herbs instead
- Saturated fats: <10% of total energy; replace with unsaturated fats
- Trans fats: <1% of total energy; eliminate industrially produced trans fats (partially hydrogenated oils)

SPECIAL DIETARY CONSIDERATIONS:
- Diabetes: Low glycaemic index (GI) foods; control carbohydrate portions; consistent meal timing; limit sugary drinks
- Hypertension: DASH diet — high in fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, whole grains, lean protein; low in sodium, red meat, sweets
- Iron deficiency: Iron-rich foods (red meat, lentils, dark green vegetables) with vitamin C (orange, tomato) to enhance absorption; avoid tea/coffee immediately after iron-rich meals
- Vitamin D deficiency (very common in Pakistan): Sun exposure 15–20 minutes midday on arms/face; dietary sources (fatty fish, fortified milk); supplementation if deficient

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES (WHO 2020)
════════════════════════════════════════
ADULTS (18–64 years):
- Minimum: 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity/week
  OR 75–150 minutes vigorous-intensity aerobic activity/week
- Muscle-strengthening: ≥2 days/week (resistance training, yoga, bodyweight exercises)
- Reduce sedentary time; any physical activity better than none

OLDER ADULTS (65+):
- Same as adults above; add balance exercises ≥3 days/week (fall prevention)
- Multicomponent physical activity prioritized

CHILDREN AND ADOLESCENTS (5–17 years):
- ≥60 minutes moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily
- Mostly aerobic; muscle and bone strengthening ≥3 days/week
- Limit recreational screen time to <2 hours/day (WHO, AAP)

BENEFITS: Reduces risk of cardiovascular disease (35% reduction), Type 2 DM (40%), depression (30%), breast/colon cancer, obesity; improves sleep quality, bone health, mental health, cognitive function

MENTAL HEALTH GUIDELINES
════════════════════════════════════════
WHO promotes mental health as integral to overall health — "No health without mental health."

PROMOTING MENTAL WELL-BEING:
- Regular physical activity (reduces depression and anxiety)
- Adequate sleep: Adults 7–9 hours/night; teenagers 8–10 hours; children 9–12 hours
- Social connections and support networks
- Stress management techniques: Mindfulness meditation, deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation
- Healthy diet (brain health)
- Limiting alcohol and avoiding illicit drugs
- Meaningful occupation and purpose

MENTAL HEALTH FIRST AID (mhGAP):
Key principles for helping someone in mental health crisis:
1. Listen actively and without judgment
2. Reassure and provide information
3. Encourage professional help
4. Avoid harmful advice (e.g., "just cheer up")
5. Do not leave alone if at risk of self-harm
6. Contact crisis services if immediate danger

STIGMA REDUCTION: Mental health conditions are medical conditions. Seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness.

MATERNAL AND CHILD HEALTH (MCH)
════════════════════════════════════════
ANTENATAL CARE (ANC):
WHO recommends ≥8 antenatal contacts during pregnancy:
- First contact: Before 12 weeks — booking visit, blood tests (CBC, blood group, Rh, hepatitis B, syphilis, HIV), urine dipstick, blood pressure
- Routine monitoring: Weight, BP, fetal growth, fetal heart rate at each visit
- Supplements: Folic acid 400mcg from preconception to 12 weeks (5mg if high risk); Iron + folic acid throughout pregnancy; Calcium supplementation in high-risk areas; Vitamin D
- Ultrasound: Dating scan 11–14 weeks; anomaly scan 18–22 weeks
- Gestational diabetes screening: 24–28 weeks (OGTT)
- Group B Streptococcus swab: 35–37 weeks
- Tetanus toxoid vaccination: Td booster if not previously immunized

DANGER SIGNS IN PREGNANCY (seek immediate care):
- Severe headache + visual disturbance + upper abdominal pain (pre-eclampsia/eclampsia)
- Heavy vaginal bleeding (antepartum haemorrhage)
- Absent or significantly reduced fetal movements
- Rupture of membranes before 37 weeks
- Severe vomiting preventing adequate nutrition
- Fever >38°C
- Signs of DVT (leg swelling, pain, warmth)
- Difficulty breathing

POSTNATAL CARE:
- Breastfeeding: Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months (WHO recommendation)
- Newborn: Vitamin K injection at birth; newborn screening; BCG and Hep B within 24 hours
- Postnatal depression: Screen all mothers with Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale; treat promptly

SEXUAL AND REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH
════════════════════════════════════════
CONTRACEPTION:
- Combined oral contraceptive pill (COCP): >99% effective if taken correctly. Reduces menstrual pain and PMS. Contraindicated in: smokers over 35, history of VTE/stroke/MI, migraine with aura, liver disease, uncontrolled hypertension.
- Progestogen-only pill (mini-pill): Suitable for breastfeeding women, those who cannot take estrogen.
- IUCD (Copper IUD): Non-hormonal, 10-year protection, also used as emergency contraception within 5 days.
- Injectable (DMPA/Depo-Provera): 3-monthly injection, convenient.
- Condom: Only contraceptive method that also protects against STIs.
- Emergency contraception (morning-after pill/Plan B): Levonorgestrel 1.5mg single dose ideally within 72 hours (effective up to 120 hours/5 days); reduces risk by ~85%.

SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED INFECTIONS (STIs):
Prevention: Consistent condom use; mutual monogamy; STI testing in new relationships; HPV vaccination; Hepatitis B vaccination; PrEP for HIV prevention in high-risk individuals.

Common STIs and treatment:
- Chlamydia: Azithromycin 1g single dose or doxycycline 100mg BD x 7 days
- Gonorrhoea: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM single dose
- Syphilis: Benzathine penicillin G (stage-dependent)
- Herpes (HSV): Aciclovir 400mg TDS x 5 days (no cure but suppresses outbreaks)
- HIV: Antiretroviral therapy (ART) — test + treat; undetectable = untransmittable (U=U)

OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH
════════════════════════════════════════
ERGONOMICS (office/computer work):
- Monitor at eye level, arm's length away
- Chair adjusted so feet flat on floor, knees at 90°
- Take a break every 30–60 minutes (20-20-20 rule for eyes: every 20 min, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds)
- Stretch neck, shoulders, wrists regularly

HEALTHCARE WORKER SAFETY:
- Hand hygiene: 5 moments (WHO) — before patient contact, before aseptic procedure, after body fluid exposure, after patient contact, after touching patient surroundings
- Standard precautions: Gloves, aprons, eye protection as appropriate for all patients
- Needlestick protocol: Wash with soap and water; report immediately; HIV PEP within 72 hours if indicated
- Vaccination: Hepatitis B, annual influenza, COVID-19

TELEMEDICINE GUIDELINES
════════════════════════════════════════
Telemedicine is the delivery of healthcare services using electronic means over a distance.

APPROPRIATE FOR TELEMEDICINE:
- Follow-up consultations for stable chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension review)
- Mental health consultations (counseling, CBT)
- Medication management and prescription renewals
- Minor illness assessment (colds, UTIs, rashes)
- Laboratory results interpretation
- Health education and lifestyle counseling
- Post-operative follow-up (when healing is expected)

NOT APPROPRIATE FOR TELEMEDICINE (requires in-person visit):
- Emergency situations (chest pain, stroke, trauma)
- Physical examination required for diagnosis
- Procedures (injections, biopsies, suturing)
- New complex symptoms requiring full workup
- Controlled substance prescriptions (in most jurisdictions)

PATIENT RESPONSIBILITIES:
- Have accurate medical history and medication list available
- Good lighting and quiet environment for video consultations
- Stable internet connection
- Have blood pressure cuff, thermometer, pulse oximeter if possible for monitoring

TELEMEDICINE DISCLAIMER:
AI-based telemedicine tools like MedGPT provide general health information and preliminary guidance. They do not replace in-person medical evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional. Always consult a licensed physician for diagnosis and treatment decisions. In emergencies, call 1122 immediately.
